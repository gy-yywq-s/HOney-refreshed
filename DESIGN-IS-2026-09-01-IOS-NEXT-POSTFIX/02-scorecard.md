   Justification: These are clear improvements rather than minor imitation, so not 1; no five-plus peer comparison establishes a genuinely new pattern, so not 3.

2. Good design makes a product useful — Score: 3/3
   Evidence: Current/next lesson remains the first substantive Home task; every fetched option remains reachable without search, and Timetable always carries teacher context (§E1, §E2, §E5).
   Justification: The primary orientation task completes in the fewest possible steps without decoys, matching 3; Access repair and completeness protocol are adjacent load-bearing defects, not a detour in the primary lesson task.

3. Good design is aesthetic — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Four semantic palettes and status-chip contrast are coherent, while broad one-off spacing, placeholder identity, and absent current rendered evidence remain (§E4).
   Justification: A visible system rules out 1; the unfinished spacing/identity/runtime-validation families prevent the orphan-free 3.

4. Good design makes a product understandable — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Explore state wording, deletion scope, direct retry, and credential-repair naming improved, while `Save and check`, indirect Access routing, `Yours`, and technical Appearance copy still need clarification (§E3, §E5, §E6).
   Justification: One load-bearing repair control family still needs clarification, so not 3; the primary controls remain identifiable and the remaining smaller wording issues do not create the two-to-three primary-control failure required for 1.

5. Good design is unobtrusive — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Search is optional, chips are quiet, Home atmosphere remains background-only, and idle decoration is zero, but visible card/list/segmented chrome remains (§E4, §E7, §E8).
   Justification: Decoration does not compete with content, ruling out 1; chrome is visible though quiet, so not 3.

6. Good design is honest — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: Deletion, palette, Portal, draft, teacher, and physical-action copy now map well; however, new credentials may be accepted through an old session, the entity source has an unreported 500-item cap, and Retry can call stale choices complete (§E2, §E3, §E5).
   Justification: Multiple material claim-to-behavior mismatches rule out 2 or 3; no forced continuity, hidden cost, scarcity, confirmshaming, or intentional deception was found, so not 0.

7. Good design is long-lasting — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Semantic Apple typography, native controls, adaptive appearance, and restrained motion are durable; the raster placeholder identity remains the explicit temporary marker (§E4, §E8).
   Justification: The single temporary identity marker prevents 3; no second or third dated trend marker supports 1.

8. Good design is thorough down to the last detail — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Loading, error, partial, filtered empty, genuine empty, success, focus, disabled, and retry states are present, but credential replacement, retry-in-flight freshness, and Access first/combined-failure recovery remain rough (§E2, §E3, §E6).
   Justification: One cross-cutting recovery/freshness state family remains rough, matching 2; the extensive state vocabulary rules out 1 or 0.

9. Good design is environmentally friendly — Score: 3/3
   Evidence: Native initial JS and idle attention are zero, adaptive appearance and Reduce Motion are honored, and Timetable/Experience repositories cache and coalesce work (§E7).
   Justification: The native equivalents of every 3-level signal are met; remaining repeated reads are bounded user-driven work rather than always-on motion or excessive initial payload.

10. Good design is as little design as possible — Score: 2/3
    Evidence: Lesson-first Home and local complete-list filtering are restrained; technical helper copy and simultaneous duplicate recovery instructions remain removable (§E1, §E5, §E8).
    Justification: At most two removable elements appear together on a representative surface, matching 2; no surface is dominated by three-to-five decorative or duplicate affordances.
