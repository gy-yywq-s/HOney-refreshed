# Design Is — Round 2 scorecard

1. Good design is innovative — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: The app refreshes a conventional Ionic school utility with a raw-voice feed, resonance-not-ranking reactions, complete context browsing, and identity-separated publication (`01-evidence.md#structural-and-flow-evidence`).
   Justification: This is a clear improvement on familiar feed/composer patterns, but not evidence of a pattern absent from five or more peer products.

2. Good design makes a product useful — Score: 3/3
   Evidence: School-day orientation and all primary destinations are visible on compact Home; Home → Timetable/Experiences/Compose is one activation (`01-evidence.md#structural-and-flow-evidence`).
   Justification: The primary tasks complete in the fewest practical steps without a decoy action.

3. Good design is aesthetic — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: One restrained paper/ink/blue system renders without overflow, but the desktop Explore searchbar breaks the 710px spine and Composer has one orphan serif heading (`01-evidence.md#visual-evidence`).
   Justification: Two minor inconsistencies prevent a completely unified visible system.

4. Good design makes a product understandable — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Main navigation and reactions are text-labelled, but the per-post Add/More duplication and icon-only notes/add actions leave one first-time-use ambiguity (`01-evidence.md#structural-and-flow-evidence`).
   Justification: One control family still benefits from tooltip-level clarification/consolidation.

5. Good design is unobtrusive — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Feed content is the figure, with rules instead of card walls and zero idle animation; persistent rail/tab chrome and fixture disclosure remain visible (`01-evidence.md#visual-evidence`).
   Justification: Chrome is quiet and non-competing, but still visibly frames the content.

6. Good design is honest — Score: 3/3
   Evidence: Fixture, draft persistence, publish/control-key separation, cooldown recovery, external processing, retention, region, and network limits map to implemented states (`01-evidence.md#copy-and-honesty-evidence`).
   Justification: No audited claim, badge, or primary action contradicted actual behavior.

7. Good design is long-lasting — Score: 3/3
   Evidence: System sans typography, 2px surfaces, hairline rules, a narrow accent palette, exact legacy wordmark, and no ambient trend effects remain (`01-evidence.md#visual-evidence`).
   Justification: The visual language contains no dated marker that should age within three years.

8. Good design is thorough down to the last detail — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: All six required states exist and main/skip/feed-target regressions are tested, but past timetable metadata fails contrast, two control classes miss 44px, and overlay focus behavior is unverified (`01-evidence.md#accessibility-and-state-evidence`).
   Justification: The state model is complete, but at least one representative state remains rough.

9. Good design is environmentally friendly — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Cold JS is 295,219 encoded bytes, with zero idle motion, dark mode, reduced-motion, route chunks, and safe update caching (`01-evidence.md#performance-and-environmental-evidence`).
   Justification: It fits the rubric's under-500KB, motion-gated level but not the under-100KB ceiling for 3.

10. Good design is as little design as possible — Score: 2/3
    Evidence: The initial screen has no overlay/badge/animation noise, but the same per-post compose destination appears both directly and in More (`01-evidence.md#structural-and-flow-evidence`).
    Justification: One clearly removable simultaneous affordance remains.

## Total

**23/30**
