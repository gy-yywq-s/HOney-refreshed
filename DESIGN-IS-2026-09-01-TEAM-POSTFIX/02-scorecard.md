# HOney iOS independent team post-fix scorecard

1. Good design is innovative — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Target-bound recovery, exact-door Access, adaptive Surface families, bounded Portal attempts, and app-wide Timetable cache improve familiar native patterns (§E2, §E7).
   Justification: These are clear improvements beyond minor imitation, so not 1; no five-plus peer comparison establishes a genuinely novel pattern, so not 3.

2. Good design makes a product useful — Score: 3/3
   Evidence: The primary current/next-lesson task is immediate and complete, while Portal, Timetable, recovery, Access, and settings routes are directly supported (§E2, §E7).
   Justification: The primary task completes in the fewest steps without a decoy; residual stale-permit and reentrant-action edges are serious secondary-state defects but do not add a detour to the primary lesson task, so 2 is not the matching anchor.

3. Good design is aesthetic — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: One coherent native type/spacing system and four contrast-tested semantic Surface families exist, but the production wordmark/small mark and current palette screenshots are unfinished (§E1, §E3).
   Justification: A visible system rules out 1; the explicit placeholder asset and unverified alternate-palette runtime prevent the complete orphan-free system required for 3.

4. Good design makes a product understandable — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Runtime/recovery/deletion/Access states are explicit, but the unfamiliar icon-only My Posts control and `Share a lesson` wording still need visible clarification (§E4, §E5, §E7).
   Justification: One materially unclear control family keeps the design below 3; the primary action and most controls are identifiable, so the two-to-three unclear-control 1 anchor is not met.

5. Good design is unobtrusive — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Login is flat and quiet, Home atmosphere is shallow, and Portal/recovery chrome appears only while needed, though outlined cards/action groups remain visible (§E1, §E3, §E7).
   Justification: Decoration does not compete with content, ruling out 1; chrome remains visible rather than fully receding, so not 3.

6. Good design is honest — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: Draft persistence, recovery read/cleanup, session/credential storage, cross-session notice, anonymity wording, and stale-permit rows still contain multiple claim-to-behavior mismatches (§E4).
   Justification: Multiple material mismatches rule out 2 or 3; no forced continuity, hidden cost, fake scarcity, confirmshaming, or intentionally deceptive flow was found, so the exact 0 anchor is not met.

7. Good design is long-lasting — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Native controls, adaptive appearance, restrained color, and semantic Surface families are durable; the placeholder raster wordmark is one explicit temporary marker (§E3, §E7).
   Justification: The single temporary brand marker prevents 3; no second or third dated trend marker supports 1.

8. Good design is thorough down to the last detail — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: All six generic and many advanced states exist, but Portal reset, Timetable debounce, Access mutation/stale-permit, and persistence edge states remain rough, with signed-in runtime still unverified (§E4, §E5).
   Justification: Two to three representative state families remain missing or behaviorally rough, matching 1; extensive explicit state handling rules out the four-plus-missing/default-browser 0 anchor.

9. Good design is environmentally friendly — Score: 3/3
   Evidence: Native initial JS and decorative idle attention are zero, dark mode and Reduce Motion are honored, Portal work is attempt-bounded, and Timetable uses caching/coalescing (§E6, §E7).
   Justification: Every exact 3-level signal is met; bounded on-demand Portal/Timetable work does not match the larger or always-on-motion 2/1 anchors.

10. Good design is as little design as possible — Score: 2/3
    Evidence: Targetless composition is gone and new retry/recovery controls are state-bearing; Home still duplicates permanent Access and Experiences destinations (§E2, §E7).
    Justification: At least two elements can be consolidated, so not 3; the representative surface is not dominated by the three-to-five removable elements required for 1.

## Total

**20/30**

The score applies the lower-score tie-breaker and worst-instance rules. No principle scores 0. The result remains below the project’s 22/30 acceptance target.
