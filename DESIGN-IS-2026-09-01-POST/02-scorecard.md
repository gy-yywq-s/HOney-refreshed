# HOney iOS post-redesign scorecard

1. Good design is innovative — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: The redesign refreshes a standard SwiftUI student companion with lesson-first Home, target-bound sharing, semantic adaptive color, and verified-door Access (§E1–§E2).
   Justification: These are clear improvements to familiar patterns, but they do not establish a demonstrably novel pattern across five or more peer products.

2. Good design makes a product useful — Score: 3/3
   Evidence: The primary school-day state is first, sharing is target-bound, cross-tab actions reuse the existing shell, and physical Access uses verified returned doors (§E2).
   Justification: The core tasks now complete directly without decoy actions or guidance-only dead ends.

3. Good design is aesthetic — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Login light/dark captures and signed-in source share one semantic type/color/surface system, but the final wordmark/mark and authenticated visual captures are still pending (§E1).
   Justification: The visible system is coherent; the temporary brand asset and source-inferred signed-in surfaces are two material but bounded incompletions.

4. Good design makes a product understandable — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Actions now name their destination and scope, Access uses actual door names, and internal jargon was removed from visible copy (§E2–§E3).
   Justification: Primary controls are predictable, while the product term “Experiences” still benefits from its explanatory heading rather than being fully self-explanatory alone.

5. Good design is unobtrusive — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Login uses flat canvas/surfaces with accent only for function, and Home’s sole shallow atmosphere remains behind content (§E1).
   Justification: Chrome is quiet, though surfaces and outlined action groups remain visibly present rather than disappearing completely behind content.

6. Good design is honest — Score: 3/3
   Evidence: Failed requests no longer become success/empty states; sync retry, deletion, disconnect, credentials, permit state, and gate names match verified behavior (§E3).
   Justification: Final delta review found no remaining load-bearing label, state, or behavior mismatch.

7. Good design is long-lasting — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: System typography, flat semantic surfaces, native controls, adaptive appearance, and restrained color replace the legacy gradient/glass/serif grammar (§E1, §E4).
   Justification: The foundation is durable, but the temporary raster wordmark is one explicit unresolved marker.

8. Good design is thorough down to the last detail — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: All required states, explicit focus, adaptive appearance, 44-point targets, labels, and Reduce Motion coverage exist; authenticated runtime accessibility remains untested (§E4).
   Justification: One important verification class—signed-in on-device accessibility and layout—is still rough by absence of runtime evidence.

9. Good design is environmentally friendly — Score: 3/3
   Evidence: Native initial JavaScript is zero, no idle animation or attention load exists, dark mode is honored, motion is gated, and third-party runtime packages are absent (§E5).
   Justification: The native app meets the rubric’s functional resource/attention signals; Debug bundle size is not treated as web initial-JS weight or App Store size.

10. Good design is as little design as possible — Score: 2/3
    Evidence: Duplicate nested navigation and targetless sharing were removed, while a small set of repeated state-exclusive actions and filters remains across the multi-screen app (§E2, §E4).
    Justification: At most two affordance families per representative surface remain candidates for consolidation without breaking the task.

## Total

**23/30**
