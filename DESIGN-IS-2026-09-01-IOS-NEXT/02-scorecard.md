# HOney iOS post-review scorecard

1. Good design is innovative — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Native patterns are materially improved by anonymous device-key control, bounded Portal recovery, actor caches, and complete non-search-gated browsing (§E2, §E6, §E8).
   Justification: This refreshes familiar patterns with clear domain improvements, so not 1; no comparison against five-plus peers proves a new interaction pattern, so not 3.

2. Good design makes a product useful — Score: 3/3
   Evidence: Current/next lesson is Home's first substantive object; every Explore category and every Timetable teacher state is directly represented (§E1, §E2).
   Justification: The primary school-day orientation task completes in the fewest possible steps without a decoy, matching 3; course composition and retry friction are adjacent tasks rather than detours in the primary task.

3. Good design is aesthetic — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: A coherent semantic system and four independently tuned palettes exist, but untokenized spacing, two dark-palette error-chip contrast failures, placeholder identity, and absent current screenshots remain (§E3).
   Justification: The visible system rules out 1; the two representative inconsistency families and unfinished runtime validation prevent the orphan-free 3.

4. Good design makes a product understandable — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: Explore completeness, `Reconnect in Settings`, local-data deletion scope, `blue accent`, and `OASIS` each create a control or wording ambiguity (§E4).
   Justification: At least three user-facing controls or outcomes need clarification and jargon is present, matching 1 rather than the single-tooltip 2 anchor; primary lesson and teacher actions remain identifiable, so not 0.

5. Good design is unobtrusive — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: There is no idle decoration or startup modal/badge, and Home's shallow gradient stays behind content, but card, list, segmented, and selected-state chrome remains visible (§E1, §E3, §E6).
   Justification: Decoration does not compete with content, ruling out 1; chrome is quiet but still visibly authored, so not 3.

6. Good design is honest — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: Import, publication, privacy, and physical-action copy is precise, but multiple label-to-behavior mismatches remain in Explore, recovery routing, deletion scope, palette wording, and local draft scope (§E4).
   Justification: Multiple material mismatches rule out 2 or 3; no forced continuity, hidden cost, scarcity, confirmshaming, or intentionally deceptive flow was found, so not 0.

7. Good design is long-lasting — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Semantic Apple typography, native controls, adaptive appearance, and low-attention motion are durable; the raster placeholder identity is the one explicit temporary marker (§E3, §E5, §E8).
   Justification: One temporary/datable identity marker prevents 3; no second or third dated trend marker supports 1.

8. Good design is thorough down to the last detail — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Empty, loading, error, success, focus, and disabled states exist, but the Explore metadata acquisition family does not distinguish loading, failure, partial, and genuinely empty (§E5).
   Justification: One representative state family remains rough, matching 2; the complete shared state vocabulary and advanced Portal/Access/persistence states rule out 1 or 0.

9. Good design is environmentally friendly — Score: 3/3
   Evidence: Native initial JS is N/A, idle attention is zero, dark mode and Reduce Motion are honored, and Timetable/Experience repositories cache and coalesce work (§E6).
   Justification: The native equivalents of every 3-level signal are met; remaining uncached reads are bounded, user-driven work rather than always-on motion or excessive initial payload.

10. Good design is as little design as possible — Score: 2/3
    Evidence: The lesson-first shell and complete grouped lists are restrained, while two explanatory/meta lines and two simultaneous duplicate-action pairs can be removed after the behavior becomes self-evident (§E1, §E8).
    Justification: At most two removable elements appear together on a representative surface, matching 2; the product is not dominated by three-to-five simultaneous decorative or duplicate affordances.

## Total

**20/30**

The lower-score tie-breaker and worst-instance rules were applied. No principle scores 0. The mechanical verdict is REFINE, but the project-specific 22/30 acceptance threshold has not been met.
