# Design Is — Round 1 Scorecard

1. **Good design is innovative — Score: 2/3**
   Evidence: Responsive rail/tab composition, feed-first identity-free stream,
   and framed context composer refresh established social and school-dashboard
   patterns; see `01-evidence.md#structural-evidence`.
   Justification: The implementation combines familiar patterns into a clear
   improvement, but there is no evidence it introduces a pattern absent from
   five or more peers.

2. **Good design makes a product useful — Score: 3/3**
   Evidence: Home orientation, Experiences browsing, entity exploration,
   private keeping, and sharing are directly reachable without decoy actions;
   see `01-evidence.md#structural-evidence`.
   Justification: The primary orient/browse/share task completes through the
   shortest credible path, while the lesson-picker gap is a completeness defect
   rather than an extra step in the primary entity flow.

3. **Good design is aesthetic — Score: 1/3**
   Evidence: The core editorial system is coherent, but the compact banner
   collision, desktop rail/duplicate-brand inconsistency, and Explore width
   discontinuity are three visible exceptions; see
   `01-evidence.md#visual-evidence`.
   Justification: Three visible inconsistencies trigger the 1-point anchor even
   though the underlying palette, typography, and ruled stream are strong.

4. **Good design makes a product understandable — Score: 2/3**
   Evidence: Primary controls are plainly labeled, but the picker promises an
   unavailable lesson list, “Find a context” is abstract, and visible reaction
   labels collapse into bare counts; see
   `01-evidence.md#copy-privacy-and-honesty-evidence`.
   Justification: The primary actions remain identifiable, but one interaction
   family needs clarification and label/behavior alignment.

5. **Good design is unobtrusive — Score: 2/3**
   Evidence: Rules and whitespace keep content dominant, while the fixture
   strip and duplicate desktop branding keep chrome visibly present; see
   `01-evidence.md#visual-evidence`.
   Justification: Chrome is quiet and functional, but not fully recessive.

6. **Good design is honest — Score: 1/3**
   Evidence: Fixture, anonymity, Access, report, and network claims are precise,
   but draft-save status, cooldown recovery, ownership-key persistence, and
   external-provider disclosure do not map 1:1 to behavior; see
   `01-evidence.md#copy-privacy-and-honesty-evidence`.
   Justification: Multiple concrete label/state mismatches require the 1-point
   anchor despite unusually strong privacy language elsewhere.

7. **Good design is long-lasting — Score: 3/3**
   Evidence: The live implementation uses the approved artwork, flat neutral
   surfaces, restrained blue, 2px geometry, no gradients, no glass, and no
   decorative motion; see `01-evidence.md#visual-evidence`.
   Justification: No dated trend marker remains, and the visual grammar should
   read as current several years from now.

8. **Good design is thorough down to the last detail — Score: 2/3**
   Evidence: Empty/loading/error/success/focus/disabled states exist, but the
   faint text token fails contrast, the skip link is unreliable with retained
   routes, main landmarks duplicate, and small feed targets remain; see
   `01-evidence.md#state-evidence` and
   `01-evidence.md#accessibility-evidence`.
   Justification: One required state family—focus/accessibility—is materially
   rough, so the design cannot receive full detail credit.

9. **Good design is environmentally friendly — Score: 2/3**
   Evidence: Initial JS is 305,258 bytes gzip, with zero idle animation, dark
   tokens, and reduced-motion support; see
   `01-evidence.md#weight-pwa-and-runtime-evidence`.
   Justification: It satisfies the rubric’s under-500KB and motion-gated anchor,
   but not the under-100KB level.

10. **Good design is as little design as possible — Score: 2/3**
    Evidence: The shell and continuous feed are restrained, but duplicate
    desktop branding and repeated share invitations are removable affordance
    patterns; see `01-evidence.md#structural-evidence`.
    Justification: The design is close to essential, with at most two
    removable patterns rather than decoration dominating the task.

## Total

**20/30**
